# Gene-universe expansion for the L2/3 cross-species loading CCA

*Scripts: `it_evo/26` (new), `it_evo/16` (universe switch), `it_evo/24` (figures).
Plan: `plan/it_evo/09.gene_universe_expansion.md`.*

## Question

Script 24's mouse-vs-human gene-loading plots ran on **357 genes**, only **51** of which
carried a mouse archetype label (A=20, B=8, C=23). Both numbers looked arbitrarily small.
Why, and does widening the gene set help?

## Why 357

Not a marker-selection step — a double HVG intersection:

```
top-2000 human L2/3 HVGs (02)  ∩  1-to-1 orthologs  ∩  top-2000 mouse L2/3 HVGs (it/19)
```

Measured (`26.L23_gene_universe.tsv`): **12,637** 1-to-1 orthologs are detected in both
datasets. 1718 of the 2000 human HVGs and 1859 of the 2000 mouse HVGs are among them, so
two independent lists of that size would overlap in **253 genes by chance**. Observed 357
— a **1.41× enrichment**. The two HVG sets are chosen per species on different datasets,
cell counts and normalizations, and they barely agree; what survives is close to an
arbitrary sample of the ortholog universe.

The 8–23 archetype counts have the same cause and are *not* a marker-discovery problem:
195 mouse markers exist and **179 are in the 12,637-gene universe**; the double-HVG cut is
what removes 144 of them.

This was visible in the record already — 357 orthologs / r = 0.623 vs 521 / 0.598 purely
from a mouse-side HVG re-selection (`report 03` §Verification 4) — but was treated as
plumbing rather than as a limitation.

## Method: extend loadings out-of-sample, refit nothing

A varimax loading is a rescaled correlation between a gene and the **fixed** cell scores.
With `Z` the per-gene z-scored expression and `S` the saved varimax scores, the in-sample
loadings satisfy `S = Z @ L` with `L` orthonormal-columned, hence

```
L = (Zᵀ S / (n-1)) @ inv(Sᵀ S / (n-1))
```

exact for the original HVGs and defined for every other gene. Script 26 asserts this
against the stored 2000-gene TSVs before writing: **max |extended − stored| = 2.2e-6
(human), 2.0e-5 (mouse, float32)**. The PCA basis, varimax rotation, Gate-A VX sets,
archetypes and markers are all untouched — only the number of gene rows changes. This is
the move `l23_evo/{29,30}` made for regulon enrichment; it had never been applied to the
loadings side.

Four universes (`16.L23_universe_ladder.tsv`), composed from `in_human_hvg` /
`in_mouse_hvg` in the universe table:

| universe | definition | n |
|---|---|---|
| `hvg_intersect` | HVG in both species — the historical set | 357 |
| `human_hvg` | HVG in human, whatever the mouse side does | 1,718 |
| `mouse_hvg` | HVG in mouse, whatever the human side does | 1,859 |
| `hvg_union` | HVG in either species — **primary** | 3,220 |

`human_hvg` and `mouse_hvg` are the two one-sided selections and are each other's control:
together they separate "selecting on the better-powered dataset helps" (47,125 human cells
vs 4,044 mouse) from "any single-species selection works".

An `all_expressed` rung (all 12,637 orthologs) was implemented and then removed — the
loadings for every gene are still written by 26, so it can be reinstated as one `elif`,
but it diluted r furthest for the least gain and its n=12,637 Ward linkage costs ~640 MB.

## Result

```
universe        n     CCA1  z1    cv1   boot1 | CCA2  z2    cv2   boot2 stable2 | cos1  cos2
hvg_intersect   357   0.623 13.0  0.525 0.94  | 0.488 13.0  0.426 0.90  False   | 1.00  1.00
human_hvg      1718   0.424 21.6  0.357 0.95  | 0.332 21.8  0.293 0.90  False   | 0.98  0.97
mouse_hvg      1859   0.408 23.2  0.344 0.93  | 0.366 27.2  0.369 0.92  True    | 0.87  0.94
hvg_union      3220   0.358 27.7  0.312 0.95  | 0.313 31.1  0.307 0.93  True    | 0.87  0.95
```

`cv` = 5-fold blocked by co-expression module; `boot` = median |cos| over 500 gene
bootstraps (human side); `cos` = alignment of the fitted weight vector to the 357-gene
solution.

**More genes do not raise r — they lower it, monotonically in n** (0.623 → 0.424/0.408 →
0.358). Critically the *cross-validated* r falls too (0.525 → 0.312), so this is genuine
dilution, not small-n optimism: the HVG∩HVG genes carry the most conserved signal per gene,
and the rest carry real but weaker signal.

**The two one-sided universes are not interchangeable**, which is why both exist:

- `mouse_hvg` **stabilizes CCA2** (boot 0.915/0.914 → 2 stable pairs); `human_hvg` lands
  exactly on the threshold (0.905 human / 0.900 mouse) and yields only 1. CCA2 is also
  stronger there on every measure (r 0.366 vs 0.332, z 27.2 vs 21.8, cv 0.369 vs 0.293) —
  despite mouse being the *smaller* dataset. Selecting genes on the human side does not
  automatically win.
- `human_hvg` stays much closer to the historical solution (cos 0.98/0.97 vs 0.87/0.94),
  as expected: the 357-gene set is a subset of it, and both are anchored to the human HVGs
  that define the human varimax basis.
- **`human_hvg` does *not* fix the archetype-marker sparsity** — it still shows A=20, B=8,
  C=23, identical to `hvg_intersect`. Mouse markers are mouse HVGs by construction, so
  requiring their human ortholog to be a human HVG collapses back to the intersection. Only
  `mouse_hvg` and `hvg_union` reach 82/35/60. If the goal is a readable archetype marginal,
  `human_hvg` is the wrong rung.

> **`r` is a property of the gene universe, not a quality score.** 0.358 and 0.623 are not
> comparable and must never be quoted without n. Figures and the ladder TSV both carry the
> universe name for this reason.

What expansion does buy:

1. **The arbitrariness goes away.** The result no longer depends on a 1.41×-enriched
   accident of two HVG lists.
2. **Evidence roughly doubles**: z 13.0 → 27.7 (CCA1) and 13.0 → 31.1 (CCA2).
3. **CCA2 crosses the stability gate** — bootstrap median 0.90 → 0.93/0.93, so
   `16.L23_axis_cca_top_genes_union.tsv` reports **2 stable pairs** where the 357-gene file
   reported 1. CCA2's weights and genes were previously withheld by `STABLE_THRESH`.
4. **Archetype coverage 51 → 177 markers** (A 82, B 35, C 60). Archetype B's marginal KDE
   was built on 8 points and is now built on 35.

**The biology is unchanged.** Weight vectors align at cos 0.87 (CCA1) / 0.95 (CCA2). CCA1
stays human-VX2-dominated (0.908 vs 0.959) on mouse VX1 (0.89), with RORB, GRM1, SLIT3,
EPHA6, ATP2B4, CNTNAP2 still leading. CCA2 stays human VX9/VX10/VX6 (0.678/0.481/0.406 vs
0.674/0.524/0.474) and its now-released gene list — **SORCS3, KCNH7, GPC6, SGCZ, CDH20,
PLCB4, RASGRF2, BDNF, KCNH5** — is the same channel/adhesion/axon-guidance program report
03 could only describe informally while the stability gate withheld it.

This upgrades report 03's hedge. Its "stable 2D conserved subspace, with CCA1 sharply
identified and the second dimension real-but-not-orientation-pinned" was a statement about
a 357-gene estimate; on 3,220 genes the second dimension **is** orientation-pinned.

## Verification

1. **Reconstruction assertion** (script 26, fail-fast): 2.2e-6 / 2.0e-5 — passed.
2. **Refactor is inert**: `16 --tokens L23 --no-grid` at the default universe reproduces
   all 7 `16.L23_*` files **bit-identically** (`diff`), isolating the universe as the only
   changed variable.
3. **Negative control at n=3220**: destroy the ortholog pairing → blocked-CV over 5
   permutations gives |r| ≤ 0.038 on CCA1/CCA2 and Σcos²θ excess ≤ 0.006, against 0.312 /
   0.307 for the true pairing.
4. **Figures**: `24{,b}.human_mouse_L23_gene_loadings_CCA{1,2}<SUFFIX>.pdf` — all three
   archetype marginals smooth; A left-shifted and B/C right-shifted on CCA1, B left-shifted
   on CCA2.
5. Script 24 now **asserts** its displayed Pearson r equals the canonical correlation the
   p-value tests, instead of silently pairing an r from one estimator with a p from
   another. This assertion **caught a live bug**: when `human_hvg`/`mouse_hvg` were added,
   24's option map was updated but its gene-set filter was not, so both silently fell
   through to all 12,637 genes. The mismatch (|r| 0.281 vs canonical 0.305) aborted the run
   instead of emitting a plausible-looking wrong figure.

## Figure variants

| script | marginals | when to use |
|---|---|---|
| `24.viz...marginal.py` | density histogram + `gaussian_kde` per archetype | shows multimodality; KDE bandwidth (0.35) is a tuned parameter |
| `24b.viz...boxplot.py` | one box per group, **plus a gray "other" box** | no bandwidth; each archetype read against the transcriptome bulk rather than against the other archetypes |

Both take `--universe {hvg_intersect,human_hvg,mouse_hvg,hvg_union}` (default `hvg_union`)
and write suffixed PDFs, so all variants coexist. 24b also prints a Mann-Whitney U of each
archetype against the "other" bulk with a rank-biserial effect size — descriptive and
uncorrected, since the three archetypes are not independent. On `hvg_union` CCA1 that gives
mouse A rb −0.88, B +0.57, C +0.67, and on CCA2 mouse B rb −0.82: the poles the marginals
show, quantified.

## Scope and open items

- **L2/3 only.** L4 / L5IT / L6IT are untested under expansion and remain governed by
  report 03. This matters: those three were called "no identifiable axis" largely on
  bootstrap instability of 0.70–0.80, and expansion raised L2/3's CCA2 from 0.90 to 0.93.
  Whether it rescues any of them is an open question and deserves its own review — running
  `26 --tokens L4 L5IT L6IT` then `16 --ladder --tokens L4 L5IT L6IT` is all that is needed.
- **Markers were not recomputed.** Script 05's one-vs-each Wilcoxon still runs over the
  2000 mouse HVGs, so markers outside that set remain undiscovered. The bottleneck for the
  figure was the intersection, not marker discovery, and report 01 depends on the existing
  record.
- **`mouse_hvg` beating `human_hvg` on CCA2 is unexplained.** The mouse dataset is 11×
  smaller, so the naive expectation was the opposite. Two candidates worth separating: the
  mouse HVG set may simply be better matched to the archetype-bearing subspace (the
  archetypes and markers are both defined there), or the human varimax basis may already be
  saturated by its own HVGs so that selecting on them adds nothing new. Nothing here
  distinguishes these.
- The removed `all_expressed` rung is one `elif` away in `16.load_loadings` if the
  monotone-dilution trend needs its endpoint; 26 already writes loadings for all genes.
