>   `SELECTED_TFS` minus Atf6 and Smad3, which are stress TFs rather than IEGs. Counting

>   labels which is which.
> - **Q2 lives in `56` rather than in `54`/`55`.** Its table is keyed by

>   be produced by a script that knows only one catalogue. Putting it in `56` also honours
>   "rather than a new script" — no new file — without forcing a full recompute of `54`/`55`,
>   which load h5ads and convolve per-stratum PMFs to produce tables this panel only reads.

Follow-up to `plan/it/54.stratified_regulon_archetype_enrichment.md` and
`plan/it/55.gao25_stratified_regulon_archetype_enrichment.md`. Answers two questions raised once
both catalogues were in place:

1. How do we highlight the consistency between the yoo25 (`54`) and gao25 (`55`) results?
2. How should L2/3 be read against the other layers, given that they carry fewer regulons —
   is the difference power or biology?

Everything below is measured from the committed `54.*`/`55.*` tables (activating `+/+` regulons,

The through-line: `41`/`42`–`45` test with a single-stratum hypergeometric null, which makes
`log2_or` depend on the gene universe (a +0.94 to +2.09 offset, catalogue- and layer-dependent).
`54`/`55` replace that null with an expression-stratified one whose exact distribution is a
convolution of per-stratum hypergeometrics, leaving `log2_enr` 8–120× more universe-stable. The
older scripts are kept rather than retired, both as provenance and because `55`'s consolidation
gate is defined against them.

Not yet built: the re-derivation of `54b`'s provisional thresholds for the published figures
(`plan/it/54`, step 5), which was deliberately left for a decision rather than assumed. It is
now the only open item in the line.

## Q1 — Consistency between 54 and 55

### The finding

Agreement is strong, but only on the subset where both catalogues actually measure the same
thing:

| comparison | n cells | agreement |
|---|---|---|
| thick in **both** (`overlap >= 5` in each) | 28 | **Pearson r = 0.858**, Spearman 0.852, median \|Δ log2_enr\| = **0.58** |
| all shared regulon × archetype cells | 274 | Spearman 0.532 |

The gap between those rows *is* the result: the lower all-cells figure is dominated by cells
resting on 0–4 shared genes in one or both catalogues — exactly what `MASK_MIN_OVERLAP` exists
to gray out. Quote the first row, with the second as context, never the second alone.

Per layer, on shared cells:

| layer | yoo25 TFs | gao25 TFs | shared | shared cells | Spearman (>=1 thick) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L2/3 | 96 | 71 | 41 (58%) | 123 | 0.751 |
| L4 | 72 | 75 | 30 (42%) | 90 | 0.532 |
| L5IT | 33 | 48 | 8 (24%) | 16 | **−0.429** |
| L6IT | 36 | 41 | 15 (42%) | 45 | 0.691 |

### Report the attrition, because "44% replicate" understates it badly

```
yoo25 significant cells                   60
  ... regulon even EXISTS in gao25        32   (53%)   <- untestable, not contradicted
  ... and significant there               14   (44% of testable)

gao25 significant cells                   26
  ... regulon exists in yoo25             18   (69%)
  ... and significant there               14   (78% of testable)
```

Two things to say about this:

- **Non-overlap, not disagreement, is the main limit.** Only 24–58% of TF+sign keys are shared
  per layer. Half of each catalogue is simply absent from the other, so the naive replication
  fraction is measuring catalogue composition more than it is measuring agreement.
- **The asymmetry is the good news.** gao25's hits are close to a *subset* of yoo25's (78%
  confirmed vs 44% the other way) — which is what the smaller, more conservative catalogue
  should look like, and would not happen if the two were disagreeing at random.

### The strongest claim is at the programme level, not the regulon level

L2/3 B′ reproduces almost completely across catalogues:

```
L2/3 B'  yoo: Atf6 Egr1 Egr2 Egr3 Egr4 Etv5 Fos Fosb Fosl2 Irf2 Junb Mxi1 Smad3
         gao: Atf6      Egr2           Fos Fosb Fosl2      Junb Maf  Smad3
      shared: Atf6 Egr2 Fos Fosb Fosl2 Junb Smad3          <- the entire IEG core
L4  A'   shared: Rfx3 Tcf4 Zeb1
L2/3 A'  shared: Pbx1 Rfx3
L6IT C'  shared: Rfx3
```

IEG TFs account for 33% of yoo25's significant cells and 27% of gao25's. That is a statement
that survives the choice of regulon catalogue, and is the one to lead with.

### What to build — **built**

`scripts/it/56.cross_catalogue_concordance.py`, reading only `54.*`/`55.*` long tables:

1. **Jointly-thick scatter** — `log2_enr` yoo25 vs gao25, one point per shared cell, coloured by
   layer, sized by `min(overlap_y, overlap_g)`; annotate Pearson r on the jointly-thick subset
   and draw the identity line. Grey out cells thick in only one catalogue rather than dropping
   them, so the reader sees what was excluded.
2. **Replication cascade** — the attrition table above, as a figure panel or TSV, with the
   untestable fraction called out separately from the non-replicating fraction.
3. **Per-archetype shared-TF table** — `56.shared_significant_tfs.tsv`, the block shown above for
   all 11 archetypes, with an IEG flag column.

Do **not** lead with a raw hit-count comparison between the catalogues; see Q2.

As built, it writes `56.shared_cell_concordance.tsv` (all 274 shared cells, both sides,
thickness and significance flags), `56.concordance_stats.tsv` (r / rho / median |Δ| per scope
× subset), `56.replication_cascade.tsv` (overall and per layer), `56.shared_significant_tfs.tsv`
and `56.cross_catalogue_concordance.pdf` (panels 1 and 2 side by side). Significance is
imported from `54b` rather than restated, so the script cannot drift from the figures if the
provisional cutoffs are revised.

### Caveat to carry into the figure

L5IT's shared subset is **16 cells with Spearman −0.31 / −0.43**. That is noise at that n, but it
will be read as contradiction if presented without the n. Either annotate the n on every
per-layer statistic or pool L5IT into an "other layers" group.

Implemented as the first option, and it turns out to be sharper than the plan expected: L5IT
has **zero** jointly-thick cells, so its ρ rests entirely on cells one catalogue could not
measure. The legend therefore carries both counts per layer — L2/3 n=41 (17 jointly thick),
L4 n=25 (6), L5IT n=8 (**0**), L6IT n=16 (5) — which retires the caveat rather than
annotating around it.

## Q2 — L2/3 versus the other layers

### The worry is justified, but the cause is not regulon count

**Cell number is not the driver.** L4 has the *most* cells of any layer (6905 vs L2/3's 6257) and
a lower hit rate. The driver is `exp`, the expected overlap under the stratified null
(`Σ_b m_b t_b / n_b`) — a pure power quantity.

Hit rate by `exp` quartile, yoo25:

| layer | Q1 | Q2 | Q3 | Q4 | overall | median exp | median \|M\| |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| L2/3 | 0% | 2% | 11% | 23% | 10.4% | 0.83 | 189 |
| L4 | 0% | 2% | 9% | 18% | 6.0% | 0.48 | 64 |
| L5IT | 0% | 0% | 18% | 8% | 4.5% | **0.19** | 147 |
| L6IT | 0% | 8% | 14% | **27%** | **13.0%** | 0.89 | 89 |

Three conclusions:

1. **L2/3 is not special once power is matched.** In the top `exp` quartile L6IT (27%) beats it
   (23%), and L6IT has the highest overall rate of any layer. The apparent L2/3 advantage is that
   its cells sit at higher `exp`, driven mainly by its much larger marker sets (median |M| 189 vs
   L4's 64).
2. **L5IT is genuinely underpowered** in yoo25 — median `exp` 0.19, with 44% of its cells in the
   bottom quartile. That one *is* a power deficit and should be stated as such rather than
   interpreted.
3. **The layer ordering is not stable across catalogues.** In gao25 it becomes L2/3 6.6% >
   L5IT 5.2% > L4 2.7% > L6IT **0.8%** — L6IT goes from best to worst. A ranking that inverts
   when the regulon catalogue is swapped is not a biological readout, and this is the single
   strongest argument against quoting per-layer hit counts at all.

### The interpretive rule this implies

Never compare raw significant-cell counts across layers. Report `exp`-stratified rates instead.

When interpreting, keep the two halves of `exp` apart — they are not the same kind of quantity:

- **|T| (regulon size)** is a property of the SCENIC+ run, not of the layer. Pure nuisance;
  control it away without comment. Note it differs *between catalogues for the same layer* —
  median |T| in L2/3 is 36 (yoo25) vs 82 (gao25) — which is precisely why the orderings diverge.
- **|M| (marker-set size)** is partly biology: it reflects how distinct that layer's archetypes
  are. Controlling it away discards signal. Say what is being controlled and why.

### What to build — **built**

Add an `exp`-stratified panel to the existing validation outputs rather than a new script:

- `5X.validation_power_by_layer.tsv` — per (catalogue, layer, `exp` quartile): n cells, n thick,
  n significant, hit rate, median `exp`, median |M|, median |T|.
- A companion figure: hit rate vs `exp` quartile, one line per layer, two panels
  (yoo25 / gao25). The crossing lines between panels make point 3 self-evident.

Both are ~40 lines over the tables that already exist.

Built as `56.validation_power_by_layer.tsv` (40 rows: 2 catalogues × 4 layers × {all, Q1–Q4})
and `56.validation_power_by_layer.pdf`, with three additions the plan did not specify:

- **The quartiles are cut over the whole catalogue, not within each layer.** This is what
  makes conclusion 1 valid — with common edges, comparing layers inside a quartile compares
  them at matched power, which per-layer edges would not (L5IT's top quartile starts below
  L6IT's median). The corollary is that a quartile is comparable across layers but *not*
  across catalogues, since the edges differ; only the overall rates are. The edges are
  written to the table and printed on the figure's x-axis so this cannot be misread.
- **`frac_of_layer_cells`**, which is the direct evidence for conclusion 2: yoo25 L5IT puts
  **44%** of its cells in the bottom quartile against L2/3's 14%.
- **`hit_rate_thick`** = significant / thick, alongside `hit_rate` = significant / all cells
  (the plan's). Since the star criterion contains `overlap >= 5`, a cell that is not thick
  cannot be called at any effect size; `hit_rate_thick` is how often a cell that *could*
  have been called was. It reads very differently — yoo25 L2/3 is 10.4% of all cells but
  50.8% of callable ones.

Each quartile marker on the figure is annotated with its own n significant / n cells, because
several rest on very few cells (gao25 L5IT Q2 is 3/19, yoo25 L5IT Q3 is 2/11) and the
zig-zag those produce would otherwise read as structure.

## Scope note

This plan documents analysis that has already been run (the numbers above are measured, not
projected). Nothing here is implemented as a script yet. The numbering follows the repo
convention that a plan shares its script's name, so this is `56.*` after the script it proposes;
it is equally the 54/55 follow-up its title says it is.

Significance throughout uses 54b's **provisional** cutoffs (`fdr_strat < 0.05`,
`log2_enr > 1`, `overlap >= 5`). If those are revised, every count in this document moves,
though the three Q2 conclusions are about *relative* rates and should survive.
