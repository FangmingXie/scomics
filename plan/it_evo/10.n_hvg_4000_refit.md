# Refit the L2/3 axis pipeline at N_HVG = 4000 and reproduce 24b

## Motivation

### The HVG-overlap curve

Mouse–human ortholog overlap as a function of the per-species HVG count, computed by
re-ranking gene variance exactly as `02...py:239-241` (human, `.X` already log-normalized)
and `it/19...py:200-210` (mouse, `log2(CP10k+1)` from `.raw`) and intersecting through the
12,637 1-to-1 orthologs with nonzero variance in both datasets. Rank reproduction was
checked against the stored HVG sets: mouse 2000/2000, human 1999/2000 (one float32 tie at
the cut boundary).

```
   N   overlap  chance  enrich   union   markers on overlap  A/B/C
 500        88    14.7   6.00      776        36            19/ 4/13
1000       140    60.3   2.32     1609        42            19/ 5/18
1500       207   139.6   1.48     2452        46            19/ 7/20
2000       357   252.7   1.41     3220        51            20/ 8/23   <- current
2500       579   395.9   1.46     3898        52            20/ 8/24
3000       900   570.4   1.58     4473        55            20/10/25
4000      1641  1003.9   1.63     5486        61            23/12/26   <- target
5000      2485  1546.3   1.61     6360        67            25/14/28
6000      3374  2203.7   1.53     7184        74            29/16/29
8000      5288  3825.1   1.38     8620        94            35/22/37
16526    11952 11952.0   1.00    12637       179            83/36/60
```

Three facts drive this plan:

1. **Overlap is a product, not a sum.** At N=2000 each species covers only ~14% of the
   ortholog universe (1718 human, 1859 mouse of 12,637), so the expected intersection is
   0.136 x 0.147 x 12637 = 253. Doubling N roughly quadruples chance overlap, which is why
   N=2000 -> 4000 multiplies the shared set 4.6x (357 -> **1641**) for 2x the genes.

2. **N=2000 sits at the minimum of the enrichment curve** (1.41). Enrichment over chance is
   6.0 at N=500, falls monotonically to its minimum at ~2000, recovers to a secondary peak
   of **1.63 at N=4000**, then decays to 1.0 as both lists approach the whole universe. The
   top ~500 HVGs are a genuinely shared highly-variable program; ranks 500-2000 fill with
   species-specific and technical variance; past ~2500 the broad shared moderately-variable
   transcriptome starts entering both lists. The historical N=2000 landed on the single
   worst point for cross-species HVG agreement relative to chance.

3. **N=4000 alone does NOT beat what we already have for the figure.** Its intersection
   carries 61 of 195 mouse markers (A=23, B=12, C=26) — still far short of the 177
   (82/35/60) that `hvg_union` already delivers at N=2000 with no refit. Markers are
   defined on the mouse side, so requiring their human ortholog to also be a top-N human
   HVG is the binding constraint, and it relaxes only slowly in N.

**Consequence: keep the universe switch.** The target is **N=4000 x `hvg_union` = 5,486
genes**, not N=4000 x `hvg_intersect`. Dropping the universe machinery in favour of the
larger N would be a regression in marker coverage.

### The concern, stated once

Raising `N_HVG` re-fits both PCAs, so it invalidates the Gate-A VX selections, the archetype
fits, the marker lists, and every score record downstream — the cascade that motivated the
out-of-sample extension in `plan/it_evo/09`. It also cannot be verified the way the
extension was: there is no "reproduces the stored record bit-for-bit" check available,
because the basis itself changes. Proceeding as asked.

## Two simplifying decisions

**1. Archetypes and markers are NOT refit.** Script `05`'s existing
`05.mouse_L23_archetype_markers.tsv` (195 markers, A/B/C) is reused as a fixed gene ->
letter lookup for colouring. The figure never uses archetype *scores*, only marker gene
sets, so nothing else is required.

This costs nothing measurable, because marker coverage on the union universe is **saturated
in N**:

```
N=2000: union=3220  markers on union=177  A/B/C=82/35/60
N=4000: union=5486  markers on union=177  A/B/C=82/35/60
N=6000: union=7184  markers on union=177  A/B/C=82/35/60
```

Variance ranking is nested (top-2000 ⊂ top-4000), so all 195 markers are mouse HVGs at any
N >= 2000, and `hvg_union` contains every mouse HVG. Every marker that can appear already
appears; refitting would add zero coloured points.

Three consequences, all simplifications:
- **The NOC question disappears.** No archetype fit means no `noc` to assume or sweep.
- **No relabelling step.** PCHA vertex order never enters, so there is no 1-to-1 matching to
  establish and no ambiguity failure mode.
- **The N=2000 vs N=4000 comparison is cleaner** — only the axes change, not the axes *and*
  the archetype definitions.

> **State this in the figure caption and the report:** the archetype labels are the N=2000
> definitions, fitted in the N=2000 mouse Gate-A subspace, held fixed while the axes are
> re-estimated at N=4000. The reading is "where do the previously-established mouse
> archetype programs sit on the newly-estimated conserved axes?". The plan therefore says
> nothing about whether the archetypes themselves are stable to gene selection — a separate
> question needing its own design.

**2. New scripts, not parameterized old ones.** Nothing existing is edited. Each new script
is a standalone copy trimmed to L2/3-only at N_HVG=4000, hardcoded. Redundancy is accepted
deliberately: it makes the N=2000 record safe **by construction** rather than by a
regression test, and each copy ends up simpler than its parent because the subclass loops,
`--compat` mode and the 16-cell grid all drop out.

Every output filename carries an `_hvg4000` tag so the two records coexist. Tags compose
with the existing universe suffix, e.g. `30.L23_axis_cca_weights_human_hvg4000_union.tsv`.

## Steps

### 1. Varimax at N=4000 — `27`, `28`

Same recipe as the parents: rank by raw variance of the log-normalized matrix, top 4000,
`StandardScaler` -> `PCA(10, random_state=0)` -> Kaiser varimax -> reorder by descending
score variance. Emit loadings, coords and both variance-partition tables, all `_hvg4000`.

Do not substitute a different HVG rule — `np.argsort(gene_var)[::-1][:N_HVG]`, not scanpy's
dispersion-binned selection.

**Gate:** the two new HVG sets must intersect through orthologs to exactly **1641** and
union to exactly **5486**. These come from the sweep above and are independent of everything
downstream. If they do not match, the ranking has drifted — stop.

> **Executed — gate revised to 1640 / 5485 (float32 vs float64), ranking confirmed intact.**
> The pipeline produced `hvg_intersect` **1640**, `hvg_union` **5485**, `human_hvg` **3450**
> (mouse_hvg 3675 matched exactly). Cause: the sweep above accumulated gene variance in
> float64, whereas `02`/`it/19` — and therefore `27`/`28`, which must copy them — cast to
> float32 first. The two rankings disagree on 3 genes at the N=4000 cut, where the relative
> variance gap is ~1e-4, below float32 accumulation error over 47k cells. Restricted to the
> ortholog universe the difference is exactly `{ANKLE2, OPRD1, ZNF697}` float64-only vs
> `{PTDSS2}` float32-only, i.e. 3450 + 3 − 1 = 3452, reconciling both figures to the gene.
> This is the same mechanism §1 already recorded at N=2000 ("human 1999/2000, one float32
> tie at the cut boundary" — that pair is `QSER1` vs `CNTNAP1`).
>
> Matching `02`'s recipe outranks matching the sweep, so `29` holds the pipeline values. What
> the gate was for — detecting *drift* — is independently confirmed: `27`'s top-4000
> reproduces the float32 ranking 4000/4000, and contains `02`'s stored top-2000 in full
> (2000/2000), so the nesting that decision 1's marker-saturation argument relies on is
> intact. Marker coverage is unaffected either way: markers are mouse genes, every one is a
> mouse HVG, and `hvg_union` ⊇ every mouse HVG regardless of the human-side boundary.

### 2. Re-derive Gate A — the judgment step

Varimax reorders components by score variance, so **VX numbering is not stable across N**.
The current `human_vx = [VX2, VX6, VX7, VX8, VX9, VX10]` and
`mouse_vx = [VX1, VX2, VX5, VX7, VX8, VX9]` are meaningless in the new basis and must not be
carried over.

Re-apply the original criterion to the new partition tables — keep VX where `cell_type` R²
dominates the nuisance factors (donor / source / library_size), drop mito/ribo-loaded axes —
and record the result in this file alongside the tables that justify it. This is a human
decision, not a script. The new sets may differ in size from six per side; nothing
downstream requires `kx == ky`.

#### Executed — the decision and the tables that justify it

**Result: `human_vx = [VX3, VX6, VX8, VX9, VX10]` (kx=5), `mouse_vx = [VX1, VX3, VX4, VX6,
VX9, VX10]` (ky=6).** So r = min(kx, ky) = 5.

The N=4000 basis is cleaner to gate than the N=2000 one: every keeper has `cell_type` as its
largest factor by ≥2.4x and every drop has a nuisance dominating by ≥2.2x, so there are no
near-ties of the kind that made human VX10 (donor 0.148 vs cell_type 0.131) and mouse VX9
(sample 0.137 vs 0.162) judgment calls at N=2000.

**One borderline call, and it turns out not to matter.** Human VX10 passes the partition test
(`cell_type` 0.196 beats every nuisance by ≥5x) but carries 9 ribosomal-protein genes in its
top-50 loadings, which invites the "drop mito/ribo-loaded axes" clause. Kept, on two grounds:

1. *Calibration against the N=2000 gate.* That gate dropped human VX3 — 24 ribo genes with
   `RPL41`/`RPL7A` at ranks 1-2 **and** donor-dominated (0.253 vs 0.023) — but kept VX8 with
   3 ribo genes and `cell_type` dominant. New VX10 is the VX8 situation, not the VX3 one: its
   top-10 is entirely biological (`DCC`, `ALCAM`, `GULP1`, `ADAMTSL1`, `GDA`, `HTR1F`,
   `ARHGAP24`, `NKAIN2`, `ZBBX`, `SORCS3`). The extra ribo content is expected — ribosomal
   genes are moderately variable, so more of them enter the HVG list at N=4000.
2. *Measured sensitivity.* `30.L23_axis_gate_sensitivity_hvg4000_union.tsv` leaves each VX out
   in turn. Dropping human VX10 moves CCA1 from **0.3531 to 0.3530** (z 37.0 -> 37.5), and
   VX10's CCA1 weight is **0.036** — it contributes essentially nothing to the headline axis.
   The keep/drop choice is not load-bearing. (For contrast, dropping human VX3 takes CCA1 to
   0.268 and mouse VX1 takes it to 0.271 — those two carry the axis.)

The remaining mito/ribo axes were all dropped by the partition test independently: human VX4
is the MT- axis (`MT-ND1/2/ATP6/ND4` top-4, donor-dominated), human VX2 has 15 mito/ribo genes
in its top-50, mouse VX2 is the mt- axis. Mouse keepers carry zero mito/ribo genes in their
top-50 and read as clean programs (`Rorb`/`Chrm2`, `Cdh13`/`Kcnq5`, `Sorcs3`/`Pcdh15`,
`Opcml`/`Sox5`, `Cux2`/`Rbfox3`). Mouse VX5 — dropped on `sample` — is the IEG/activity axis
(`Homer1`, `Nr4a3`, `Egr3`, `Per1`), which is the expected place for it to land.

### 3. Extension, CCA, figure — `29`, `30`, `31`

- `29` re-extends loadings to all expressed genes from the new varimax scores. Its
  reconstruction assertion (extended vs stored 4000-gene loadings < 1e-4) is the self-check
  that the new basis is internally consistent.
- `30` runs the CCA. Universes at N=4000: `hvg_intersect` 1641, `human_hvg` 3452,
  `mouse_hvg` 3675, `hvg_union` 5486. Primary is `hvg_union`.
- `31` plots, reading `05.mouse_L23_archetype_markers.tsv` **unchanged** — no relabelling,
  no remapping. Expect A/B/C = 82/35/60 on `hvg_union`, identical to the N=2000 figure by
  the saturation argument in decision 1; anything else means the gene set is wrong.

## Verification

1. **N=2000 record safe by construction** — no existing script is edited. A `git status`
   showing only new files is the whole check.
2. **Gene-set gate** — intersection exactly 1641, union exactly 5486 (§1).
3. **Extension assertion** — `29` passes at < 1e-4 (expect ~1e-5).
4. **Marker coverage is a fixed target, not an observation** — `31` must report A/B/C =
   82/35/60 on `hvg_union`, identical to N=2000. Decision 1 proves this analytically, so any
   deviation means the gene set is built wrong, not that the biology moved.
5. **Axis comparability across N.** Canonical *weight* vectors are **not** comparable between
   records — different VX bases. Compare the **per-gene canonical projections**: for genes in
   both records, correlate the N=4000 CCA1 gene score against the N=2000 CCA1 gene score, per
   species. High correlation means the same axis in a new basis; low means N changed the
   biology, which would be the headline.
6. **Negative control** — permute the ortholog pairing at n=5486, confirm blocked-CV -> ~0,
   as done at n=3220.
7. **Interpretive check** — the N=4000 figure differs from the N=2000 one only in its axes
   and gene set; the coloured points are the same 177 genes. Any visual change is the axis.

## Expected outcome, and how to read it

`r` will fall again — the universe grows 3220 -> 5486 and every rung of the N=2000 ladder
showed monotone dilution in n. **That is not a regression**, and `r` from the N=4000 record
must never be compared to 0.358 or 0.623. What legitimately compares across records is
bootstrap stability, blocked-CV r relative to its own permuted null, and verification 5.

## Out of scope

- **Human NOC sweep and human archetype fit** (`03`, `04`). 24b reads eight files and none is
  a human archetype product: human varimax loadings, mouse varimax loadings, 16's canonical
  weights, `05.mouse_L23_archetype_markers.tsv`, and the ortholog table. The scatter is
  coloured by *mouse* archetypes only, so the human dataset enters purely as gene loadings;
  `16...py:19` refuses PCHA products by design. Human Gate A **is** needed (§2) and is
  independent of NOC — it is chosen from the variance partition before any NOC sweep.
- L4 / L5IT / L6IT — still governed by `report/it_evo/03`.
- Score transfers and conservation (`06`-`14`, `09`) — `report/it_evo/01`'s record.
- **Refitting mouse archetypes/markers at N=4000** (a counterpart to `05`). Skipped by
  decision 1: it would add zero coloured points. Whether the archetypes are stable to the
  gene set is a real question, but it needs its own design — comparing vertex geometry and
  marker overlap across bases — not a side effect of this figure.
- Promoting N=4000 to the project default. That needs the N=4000 record in hand first, and
  would require redoing everything above plus human `03`/`04`.

**Known limitation, pre-existing:** the mouse-only colouring means 24b (and `32`) show where
the mouse archetype programs sit on the conserved axes and say nothing about the human ones.
Equally true at N=2000; the human counterpart would be a different figure and would pull
`03`/`04` back in.
