# Plan: which mouse axis best matches which human axis — CCA on ortholog gene loadings

## Context

The question: **which axis in mouse best matches which axis in human**, where an "axis" may
be a *linear mixture* of components, and "matching" means high correlation of the
orthologous-gene loadings.

### The estimator is CCA, and CCA here is exactly principal angles between subspaces

Put the two loading matrices in shared ortholog gene space:

- `X` = human loadings, `n_genes × k_human`
- `Y` = mouse loadings, `n_genes × k_mouse`

with **genes as the observations** and components as the features, rows paired by 1-to-1
orthology. Finding `a`, `b` maximizing `corr(Xa, Yb)` is CCA. The paired-two-block structure
is what makes it CCA rather than projection pursuit — PP searches a *single* dataset for
projections that are "interesting" under some index and has no second block to correlate
against.

Orthonormalize each side (thin SVD → `Qx`, `Qy`); the singular values of `Qxᵀ Qy` **are** the
canonical correlations, and equal the cosines of the principal angles between the two
gene-loading subspaces. Closed form, no NIPALS, no `x_weights_`/`x_rotations_` ambiguity.
`scipy.linalg.subspace_angles` is the same computation.

### The invariance that governs the whole design

CCA is invariant to any invertible linear transform of either block. Verified on L2/3
(`it_evo` inputs): raw loadings, column-z-scored loadings, and loadings post-multiplied by a
random 6×6 matrix all give the identical spectrum `0.6231 0.4881 0.3040 0.2301 0.1312
0.0037`, and `cos(subspace_angles)` returns the same six numbers. Three consequences that the
script and the writeup must respect:

1. **Column z-scoring is a no-op for CCA.** It affects only the pairwise-Pearson baseline.
   Keep it there; do not present it as preprocessing that changes the canonical result.
2. **The varimax rotation is irrelevant to the canonical correlations.** Unrotated PCs
   spanning the same subspace give identical numbers. Only the *weights* carry VX identity;
   the correlations are a property of the span alone.
3. **Gate-A VX selection is therefore the entire modeling decision.** It gets a first-class
   sensitivity analysis, not a footnote. In particular, enlarging the VX set enlarges the
   span, so canonical correlations increase **monotonically** — see Pilot §4.

### What already exists

`scripts/l23_evo/23.orthoaxis_corr.py` and `24.orthoaxis_cca.py` do this **for L2/3 only**,
hardcoded, on the older `l23_evo/05` + `18` loadings (521 shared orthologs):

- `23`: plain component-by-component Pearson matrix, max |r| = 0.380 (human VX2 ↔ mouse VX8),
  and not one-to-one — human VX6 and VX8 both point at mouse VX8/VX10.
- `24`: `sklearn` CCA, spectrum 0.598 / 0.411 / 0.341 / 0.221 / 0.168 / 0.039. CCA1 is human
  VX2 (0.937) against a mixture of mouse VX1 (−0.634) and VX8 (−0.687) — why the pairwise
  matrix underestimated it. Top genes GRM1, EPHA6, RORB, TRHDE read as the deep↔superficial
  L2/3 gradient.

Neither has a null model. Two implementation defects to avoid when porting: `24` back-projects
genes with `x_weights_` rather than `x_rotations_` (`24:128-129`), which diverges from the
`X_c` used for the reported correlations under deflation — this corrupts the **top-gene lists
for components ≥ 2**, though the pilot confirms the reported *correlations* were unaffected;
and its cell projection (`24:152-160`) skips sklearn's internal centering/scaling. The
closed-form route sidesteps both.

reproduce the table below exactly, so these are the numbers script `16` must reproduce.

### 2. Cross-validation contradicts the permutation, and is the finding that matters

5-fold over genes; weights fit on training genes, correlation evaluated on held-out genes,
component 1, **signed**:

| token | in-sample | CV folds | CV mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| L23 | 0.623 | 0.623, 0.588, 0.481, 0.645, 0.570 | **0.581** |
| L4 | 0.365 | 0.083, 0.178, 0.114, 0.145, 0.256 | **0.155** |
| L5IT | 0.334 | 0.184, 0.021, −0.075, 0.149, −0.010 | **0.054** |
| L6IT | 0.381 | −0.045, 0.113, 0.401, 0.138, 0.293 | **0.180** |

L2/3 is real and stable. L5IT is nothing — two folds negative. Permutation tests "the pairing
is non-random," which a diffuse shared signal satisfies; CV tests "the canonical direction is
estimable," which is the claim we actually want to make.

**But random-fold CV is not itself safe.** Simulating genes in co-expression blocks with
**zero** planted signal (n = 332, k = 5, block size ~4–17):

| independent blocks | CCA1 | z | CV, random folds | CV, folds blocked by module |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 332 (genes independent) | 0.198 | 0.0 | −0.004 | −0.004 |
| 80 | 0.351 | 4.5 | **0.231** | −0.033 |
| 40 | 0.516 | 9.7 | **0.444** | 0.016 |
| 20 | 0.678 | 14.7 | **0.641** | −0.090 |

Co-expressed genes straddle a random train/test split and leak, so random-fold CV reports a
large effect where there is none; blocking the folds by module recovers ≈ 0. **Folds must be
blocked by co-expression module, not drawn at random** — this is the single most important
correction to the CV design, and it applies to the pilot numbers above, which are
random-fold and therefore optimistic.

### 3. The overlap is multi-dimensional, and component 1 is often not the strongest

Running the permutation null **per component** rather than only for CCA1 (4000 permutations):

| token | observed spectrum | null mean | z per component | Σcos²θ obs / null |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L23 | 0.623, 0.488, 0.304, 0.230, 0.131, 0.004 | 0.217, 0.164, 0.121, 0.082, 0.046, 0.015 | **13.0**, 13.0, 8.2, 7.6, 5.0, −1.0 | 0.79 / 0.10 |
| L4 | 0.365, 0.267, 0.244, 0.070 | 0.165, 0.116, 0.076, 0.039 | 7.0, 6.7, **8.7**, 1.8 | 0.27 / 0.05 |
| L5IT | 0.334, 0.295, 0.213, 0.072, 0.025 | 0.201, 0.143, 0.096, 0.054, 0.017 | 4.1, **5.7**, 5.1, 0.9, 0.6 | 0.25 / 0.08 |
| L6IT | 0.381, 0.307, 0.173, 0.015 | 0.178, 0.114, 0.063, 0.019 | 5.7, **6.9**, 4.9, −0.3 | 0.27 / 0.05 |

For **three of four tokens the leading component is not the most significant direction** — L4
peaks at component 3, L5IT and L6IT at component 2 — and two or three components sit at
comparable z. Reporting only CCA1 hides this completely.

This is what explains the §2 dissociation, and it is not any of the obvious stories. Four
generative models simulated at these dimensions — weak-but-real single axis with iid genes,
heavy-tailed loadings with signal in 40 of 332 genes, co-expression blocks with no signal, and
two equal planted axes — all give **CV ≈ 0.22–0.23 at z ≈ 4.5**, because z and CV rise
together in each. The observed L5IT (z 4.2, CV 0.054) and L4 (z 7.1, CV 0.155, models predict
≈ 0.35) are reproduced by none of them.

The mechanism is rank degeneracy in the *ordering*: when several canonical correlations are
close, the direction ranked first on training genes is not the one ranked first on test genes,
so component-wise CV measures a rotation mismatch and collapses even though the subspaces
genuinely overlap. Σcos²θ confirms the overlap is real for every token (0.25–0.27 against a
null of 0.05–0.08 for L4/L5IT/L6IT).

Two supporting observations. Deleting L5IT's 20 most influential genes barely moves CCA1
(0.334 → 0.324) — no small gene set carries it, the signal is diffuse. L2/3 behaves oppositely:
CCA1 0.623 stands clear of CCA2 0.488, and deleting its top 10 genes (RORB, GRM1, TRHDE,
DSCAML1, GRM3, PTPRT …) drops it 0.623 → 0.530 — a dominant, gene-localized, estimable axis.

**Consequence for the design**: component-wise statistics are the wrong primary summary for
three of four tokens. Σcos²θ and subspace-level CV become primary; component-wise numbers are
reported alongside but are not what a "no conserved axis" claim rests on.

### 4. The specificity grid breaks the diagonal story

Raw CCA1 is **not comparable across the 16 cells**: null mean spans 0.165–0.217, n spans
302–479, k spans 4–6. Each cell must be scored against its own null. Even after that
(CCA1, z in parentheses):

| human \ mouse | L23 | L4 | L5IT | L6IT |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L23** | **0.623** (13.2) | 0.472 (8.2) | 0.484 (8.3) | 0.440 (7.3) |
| **L4** | 0.339 (5.4) | 0.365 (7.1) | **0.397 (7.5)** | 0.261 (3.4) |
| **L5IT** | 0.344 (4.2) | 0.280 (2.8) | 0.334 (4.2) | 0.342 (**5.0**) |
| **L6IT** | 0.394 (5.3) | 0.390 (5.6) | **0.452 (7.3)** | 0.381 (5.7) |

Only L2/3 is diagonal-dominant. Human L4 and human L6IT both match **mouse L5IT** better than
their own homolog. Note the off-diagonal is a *conservative* null — all four are IT neurons
sharing continuous laminar gradients — so a non-dominant diagonal is ambiguous between "the
diagonal is spurious" and "the shared axis is pan-IT rather than subclass-specific." The
disambiguation is committed to in advance in Verification §7.

### 5. Enlarging the VX set raises CCA1 by construction

Old L2/3 inputs, Gate-A 6 VX vs all 10 VX per side:

| VX set | CCA1 | null mean |
|---|---|---|
| Gate-A (6 × 6) | 0.598 | 0.181 |
| all (10 × 10) | **0.787** | 0.248 |

Superset span ⇒ monotone increase. The previous draft's "all-ten-VX negative control" — that
donor/libsize axes "should not contribute" — cannot fail in the direction described, because
CCA recruits them whenever they help. Reframed in Verification §6.

### 6. The canonical weights are unstable exactly where we need them

500 gene-bootstraps, |cos| between the resampled and full-data human weight vector, comp 1:

| token | median | 5th pct | frac < 0.9 |
|---|---|---|---|
| L23 | 0.943 | 0.664 | 0.27 |
| L4 | 0.797 | 0.148 | 0.75 |
| L5IT | 0.701 | 0.102 | 0.84 |
| L6IT | 0.785 | 0.217 | 0.76 |

Full-data L2/3 human weights: VX2 −0.96, VX6 0.21, VX7 −0.08, VX8 0.05, VX9 −0.14,
VX10 0.07 — i.e. essentially "human VX2", consistent with `24`. For L4/L5IT/L6IT the weight
vector is close to arbitrary and must not be interpreted. Related degeneracy: when adjacent
canonical correlations are close (L5IT 0.334/0.295, L6IT 0.381/0.307) the two directions are
rotationally interchangeable even at fixed data.

### 7. Two previously planned steps have no teeth here

Within-species loading columns are near-orthogonal — max off-diagonal |r| 0.070–0.237,
condition number 1.18–1.51 across all eight blocks. So:

- **PLS-SVD** cannot disagree with CCA: its stated rationale is catching a solution riding a
  low-variance direction, and there is no such direction. Dropped as a separate step; the
  condition number is reported instead so the reader knows why.
- **The rank-deficiency guard** never triggers; effective rank is always `k`. Dropped.

## Script `16.crossspecies_axis_matching.py`

Structure copied from `scripts/it_evo/09...py`: a `SUBCLASSES` config list, a `--tokens` CLI
arg, per-token output naming, plus a `--compat` flag for the L2/3 control.

Per subclass pair:

1. **Shared orthologs**, then closed-form CCA: thin-SVD-orthonormalize each centered block to
   `Qx`, `Qy`; `s = svd(Qxᵀ Qy, compute_uv=False)`, clipped to [0,1]. Report the full spectrum,
   the condition number of each block, and the **subspace overlap**

   ```
   Σcos²θ = Σᵢ sᵢ²  =  ‖Qxᵀ Qy‖²_F        (equivalently trace(Qxᵀ Qy Qyᵀ Qx))
   ```

   which ranges over [0, min(kx, ky)] and has expectation ≈ `kx·ky / (n − 1)` for two
   independent random subspaces (the −1 from centering). Report it raw, as a fraction of
   `min(kx, ky)`, and as its own permutation z. It is the k-invariant summary that survives
   the ordering degeneracy of Pilot §3, and it is a single `‖·‖_F` on a `k × k` matrix — no
   extra cost.
2. **Cross-validation — primary, at both the component and the subspace level.**
   K=5 folds over orthologs. On each training fold, solve the whitened problem
   (`cholesky(Sxx)`, `cholesky(Syy)`, SVD of the whitened cross-covariance) for the full
   weight matrices `A`, `B`; then on held-out genes report

   - **component-wise**: `corr(X_test a₁, Y_test b₁)` — per-fold signed values and their
     signed mean, never `mean |r|`, which would hide the negative folds that are the actual
     signal for L5IT;
   - **subspace-level**: `Σcos²θ` between `span(X_test A_train)` and `span(Y_test B_train)`,
     with the same quantity computed on the permuted pairing as its null. This is the number
     that does not collapse under the ordering degeneracy, and per Pilot §3 it is the one
     three of four tokens need.

   **Folds are blocked by co-expression module, not drawn at random** (Pilot §2). Modules are
   obtained by hierarchical clustering of the human loading matrix over the shared orthologs
   into `n_modules ≈ n/8` groups, cut at a fixed distance; genes in a module always land in
   the same fold. Report both blocked and random-fold values so the gap is visible — on the
   pilot's simulated data that gap was 0.23 vs −0.03 at zero true signal. Repeat over R=10
   module-assignment seeds and report the spread.
3. **Bootstrap weight stability.** B=500 gene-bootstraps; for each, |cos| between the
   resampled and full-data canonical weight vector, per side, for components 1–2. Report
   median, 5th percentile, and `frac < 0.9`. **Weights are only written to the interpretable
   output when the median ≥ 0.9**; otherwise the row is emitted with `stable=False` and the
   viz greys it out.
4. **Permutation null — demoted to a sanity line, but reported for every component.** Permute
   the mouse-side gene labels `N_PERM = 4000` times. Because permuting rows of `Y` commutes
   with orthonormalization (`Qy_perm = P Qy`), each replicate is a single `k × k` SVD of
   `Qxᵀ P Qy` — no re-orthonormalization; 4000 replicates run in about a second. Retain the
   **whole spectrum** per replicate, not just its first entry, and report null mean, null sd
   and z for **every component and for Σcos²θ**. Pilot §3 found the leading component is not
   the highest-z direction for three of four tokens; a CCA1-only null would have hidden that.
   `N_PERM = 1000` estimates the null mean adequately but not its sd — z moved 13.2 → 13.07
   between 1000 and 20000 replicates — so quote z to ±0.2, not ±0.01.

   Report **z, not a p-value**: the empirical p floors at 1/(N+1) and every cell is past that
   floor. Two caveats to record in the output docstring:

   - **z is a standardized effect size, not a normal deviate.** The null is bounded on [0,1]
     and right-skewed (skew +0.28 to +0.33, excess kurtosis ≈ +0.1, Shapiro p ~1e-10), with a
     much shorter right tail than a Gaussian: over 20000 permutations the *largest* null draw
     sat at z ≈ 4.4. Converting z = 13.07 to a Gaussian tail gives 2.6e-39, which is
     meaningless. Quote the empirical bound separately (0 of 20000 ≥ observed, p < 5e-5).
   - The null assumes gene exchangeability, which co-expression violates, so it is
     anti-conservative and subordinate to step 2.
5. **Gate-A sensitivity.** Re-run step 1 (a) with all ten VX per side and (b) leave-one-VX-out
   over the Gate-A set, each against its **own** permutation null. Since the spectrum is
   monotone in the VX set, the reported quantity is the *z*, not the raw correlation, and
   the leave-one-out delta is judged against the bootstrap spread from step 3.
6. **Pairwise baseline.** The `23` analog on column-z-scored loadings — the plain
   component × component Pearson matrix — so the gain from allowing mixtures is explicit
   (for old L2/3 that gain was 0.380 → 0.598).
7. **Top genes** per canonical pair by `|x_loading * y_loading|`, as `23:183-184`, emitted
   only for pairs that pass the step-3 stability gate.

### Subclass-specificity grid

Run steps 1, 2 and 4 for all **16** human × mouse subclass pairs. Because `k` and `n` vary
across cells, the grid is reported in five aligned matrices. **Σcos²θ and its blocked CV are
the interpreted pair**; the CCA1 matrices are secondary, because Pilot §3 shows CCA1 is not the
strongest direction in three of four diagonal cells:

- **Σcos²θ**, as a fraction of `min(kx, ky)` — the primary, k-invariant summary
- **z of Σcos²θ** against the per-cell permutation null
- **blocked-CV Σcos²θ** — the primary generalization statistic
- z of CCA1 (secondary; the previous draft's headline)
- raw CCA1 and component-wise blocked CV (for the record only — not comparable across cells)

Multiple testing: the grid is 16 cells × up to 6 components. Report Benjamini–Hochberg q
alongside z across the 16 **Σcos²θ** tests — one test per cell, so these are the independent
ones — and state that per-component tests within a cell are not independent of each other.

Outputs (`local_data/res/it_evo/`):

- `16.<TOKEN>_axis_cca_spectrum.tsv` — one row per component plus a `subspace` row, with
  `component, r, null_mean, null_sd, z, r_cv_blocked, r_cv_random, r_cv_folds, cond_x, cond_y`;
  the `subspace` row carries Σcos²θ, its fraction of `min(kx,ky)`, its null and z, and its
  blocked CV
- `16.<TOKEN>_axis_cca_weights_{human,mouse}.tsv` — canonical axis as a VX mixture, with
  `boot_cos_median`, `boot_cos_p5`, `stable`
- `16.<TOKEN>_axis_pairwise_corr.tsv` — the component × component baseline matrix
- `16.<TOKEN>_axis_gate_sensitivity.tsv` — all-10 and leave-one-VX-out, with z
- `16.<TOKEN>_axis_cca_top_genes.tsv` — stable pairs only
- `16.<TOKEN>_cv_modules.tsv` — the co-expression module assignment used to block the CV folds
- `16.crossspecies_axis_specificity_{cos2,cos2z,cos2cv,cca1z,cca1}.tsv` — the five 4 × 4 grids

## Verification

1. **Reproduce the pilot.** The four diagonal spectra, the per-component null and z table, the
   Σcos²θ values, the 16-cell grid, the CV means, and the bootstrap stability numbers in Pilot
   §1–§7 must come back to within Monte-Carlo noise (seeded RNG; correlations to ~±0.01,
   z to ~±0.2). This is the acceptance test for the script. Note the pilot's CV numbers are
   **random-fold**; the blocked-fold values the script reports will be lower, and that is the
   expected direction — a blocked CV that came back *higher* means the blocking is wrong.
2. **CV positive control.** Run the whole CV machinery on permuted data (destroy the ortholog
   pairing, then cross-validate). Blocked CV must return ≈ 0 for both the component-wise and
   the Σcos²θ statistic. If it does not, the folds are leaking. Run the same control on
   random folds and confirm it does *not* return 0 when co-expression modules are present —
   that inflation is the reason for blocking, and it should be visible in the record.
3. **Σcos²θ identity.** `Σᵢ sᵢ² == ‖Qxᵀ Qy‖²_F == trace(Qxᵀ Qy Qyᵀ Qx)` to 1e-10, and the
   permutation null mean for Σcos²θ must sit near `kx·ky/(n−1)` (pilot: 0.05–0.10 against
   predicted 0.05–0.11).
4. **L2/3 control against the published implementation** (`--compat`): run on the *old*
   inputs — `l23_evo/05.varimax_loadings.tsv` + `18.mouse_varimax_loadings.tsv` with
   `23`/`24`'s hardcoded VX sets, 521 shared orthologs — and confirm the spectrum
   `0.5981 0.4111 0.3412 0.2212 0.1680 0.0390`. The pilot already confirms sklearn's NIPALS
   and the closed form agree to 4 dp on **all six** components, so this check is cheap and
   expected to pass; it validates plumbing, not method. The `it_evo` record will **not**
   reproduce 0.598 — `it/19` and `l23_evo/18` select different HVGs, giving 357 shared
   orthologs rather than 521, hence 0.623.
5. **Mathematical sanity**: canonical correlations in [0,1] and non-increasing;
   `cos(subspace_angles(X, Y))` matches; the spectrum is unchanged under column z-scoring and
   under a random invertible mixing of either block (this is the regression test for the
   invariance the design rests on).
6. **Gating monotonicity, replacing the old negative control.** Confirm the all-10-VX spectrum
   dominates the Gate-A spectrum element-wise (it must, by span inclusion) — a violation means
   a bug. The *substantive* question is then whether the all-10 **z** exceeds the Gate-A z,
   and whether any donor/libsize VX carries weight exceeding the step-3 bootstrap spread. If
   it does, the match is partly tracking technical structure and the Gate-A number should be
   read as the biological one.
7. **Diagonal vs pan-IT, committed in advance.** If a human subclass's best mouse match by z
   is off-diagonal (pilot: human L4 → mouse L5IT, human L6IT → mouse L5IT), the conclusion
   is *not* "the diagonal is spurious" by default. Disambiguate by asking whether the leading
   canonical gene axis is shared across cells in a row: if the top-gene sets and weight
   vectors of a row's cells are mutually aligned (|cos| ≥ 0.9), the signal is one pan-IT
   gradient and is reported as such; if they differ, the off-diagonal win stands as evidence
   against subclass-specific axis correspondence.
8. **Cross-check against the archetype story**: the leading canonical axis for L2/3 should be
   recognizable as the deep↔superficial gradient (`24`'s top genes were GRM1, EPHA6, RORB,
   TRHDE) and should relate to the mouse→human archetype assignment `09` reported. This is
   the only subclass where the pilot says the weights are stable enough to try.

## Expected outcome, stated honestly

The pilot already fixes the shape of the answer, so this is a plan to *establish and document*
it rigorously, not to discover it:

- **L2/3 has a genuine conserved axis.** CCA1 0.623 standing clear of CCA2 0.488, z = 13.0,
  random-fold CV 0.581, Σcos²θ 0.79 against a null of 0.10, weights stable (bootstrap median
  0.94), gene-localized (deleting RORB/GRM1/TRHDE and the rest of the top 10 costs
  0.623 → 0.530), essentially "human VX2 vs a mouse VX1/VX8-type mixture", and the only
  diagonal-dominant row in the specificity grid.
- **L4, L5IT and L6IT have real subspace overlap but no identifiable axis.** Σcos²θ is
  0.25–0.27 against nulls of 0.05–0.08, so the subspaces are genuinely non-independent; but
  the overlap is spread across two or three components of comparable z (L4 peaks at component
  3, L5IT and L6IT at component 2), component-wise CV collapses to 0.155 / 0.054 / 0.180, and
  the weights are unstable under bootstrap. The honest claim is *"these subspaces share
  structure"*, **not** *"human axis X corresponds to mouse axis Y"*.
- **Where the non-L2/3 subclasses match anything, it is mouse L5IT**, not their own homolog —
  consistent with a single pan-IT gradient rather than subclass-specific axis correspondence.

The middle bullet is the one most easily mis-stated. "Fails cross-validation" is true of the
*component-wise* statistic and false of the subspace statistic; writing it up as "no shared
structure" would be wrong, and writing it up as "a conserved axis" would be equally wrong. The
distinction is the reason Σcos²θ and blocked CV are both first-class outputs.

This sharpens rather than overturns `l23_evo/24`: 0.598 was never inside its null band, but a
single well-identified axis is specific to L2/3 and does not generalize down the IT hierarchy.
The graded result for L4/L5IT/L6IT is the substantive new content, and the per-component
z/CV panel plus the Σcos²θ specificity grid are the two figures that carry it.
