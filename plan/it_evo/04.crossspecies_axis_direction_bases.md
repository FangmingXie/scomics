# Addendum to 03: the CCA1 direction expressed in the VX and PC bases

Companion to `plan/it_evo/03.crossspecies_axis_matching_cca.md`. This adds **one thing**: the
leading canonical direction written out in two coordinate systems — the Gate-A VX components,
and the PCs recomputed from those same VXs. Nothing else about 03 changes.

## The basis

For each token and species, PCA on the cell-level Gate-A VX coordinates
(`02.human_<TOKEN>_varimax_coords.tsv`, `it/{19,21,23,25}.cheng22_<TOKEN>_varimax_coords.tsv`),
**keeping all `k` components** — the same construction as the PCHA inner PCA in `04:163`/`05`,
but without its `[:ndim]` truncation.

Retaining all `k` makes this an orthogonal rotation of the VX basis, so:

- gene loadings transform as `L_pc = L_vx @ comps.T`, spanning exactly the same subspace;
- a canonical direction transforms as `a_pc = comps @ a_vx`;
- **the canonical correlations are unchanged.**

Verified on all four tokens: the VX-basis and PC-basis spectra are identical
(`np.allclose(..., atol=1e-9)` → `True`), and the basis-change identity holds to
`|⟨comps · a_vx, a_pc⟩| = 1.000000` on both species. So this document is a re-expression of
03's result, not a second analysis.

> Not to be confused with the **truncated** PCHA loadings (`04/05.*_pcha_gene_loadings.tsv`,
> `ndim = k − 1`). Those are a strict subspace and would change the numbers; 03's decision to
> exclude them stands.

Sign convention: canonical vectors are defined up to a joint sign flip. Below, both sides are
flipped together so the human weight of largest magnitude is positive — which preserves the
(positive) canonical correlation.

## Which basis to report

Neither dominates. Concentration of the CCA1 direction, by largest |weight|:

| token | human VX | human PC | mouse VX | mouse PC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L23 | **0.96** | 0.86 | **0.72** | 0.66 |
| L4 | 0.86 | **0.93** | **0.91** | 0.76 |
| L5IT | **0.80** | 0.79 | 0.62 | **0.77** |
| L6IT | 0.82 | **0.98** | 0.67 | **0.74** |

The PC basis is sparser in 4 of 8 cases, the VX basis in 3, with L5IT-human a tie. The pattern
is interpretable: the PC basis wins where the Gate-A VX components split one biological
gradient across several axes (human L6IT's VX6/VX7 pair, mouse L5IT's four-way spread), and
loses where varimax already isolated the gradient on a single VX (L2/3's VX2).

Recommendation: report **both** columns side by side in `13.<TOKEN>_axis_cca_weights_*.tsv`
rather than choosing. The transform is one orthogonal matrix, costs nothing to carry, and the
two views disagree about which components matter in a way that is itself informative — a
direction that is one VX in one species and three PCs in the other is a different claim than
one that is PC1 in both.

## Carry-over caveat from 03

Everything above is a **re-expression of a direction, not evidence that the direction is
estimable.** 03's bootstrap says only L2/3 clears the stability gate (|cos| median 0.94, both
sides); L4, L5IT and L6IT sit at 0.70–0.80 with 5th percentiles of 0.10–0.22. Changing basis
does not change that — an unstable direction is equally unstable in any coordinate system.
The L4/L5IT/L6IT rows above are recorded for completeness and must carry the `stable=False`
flag wherever they are rendered.
